# Linetake

Linetake is a voice over recorder for Mac that records a script line by line, one named WAV per line, for voice actors.

E-learning, IVR and animation clients send scripts with hundreds of lines and want each line as its own file, named to their spec. Audio editors can split a long take at markers, but every name is still typed by hand. One voice actor with 216 tracks asked how to name them from a spreadsheet, and the answer was that the editor cannot do it.

## What it does
- Opens the client's script as CSV, TSV or TXT, the first sheet of an Excel file, or the first table of a Word file. Each row is one line.
- Builds each file name from the script's columns and typed text, such as ID, an underscore and Character for M2_014_Priya.wav.
- Shows the line in large type, its direction underneath and the next line below it.
- Space starts a take. Space again saves it and moves to the next line with no take.
- Trims silence at both ends of each take to a quarter of a second.
- Records mono WAV at 44.1, 48 or 96 kHz and 16 or 24-bit, from the input and channel you pick.
- Shows which lines are recorded and which are missing, read from the files in the folder.
- Free for the first 20 lines of every script. Recording a line again moves its old take to the Trash.
- Linetake Pro, a one-time purchase of $4.99 in the US, adds:
  - Every line of any script, with no cap.
  - Each replaced take kept in an "Other takes" folder. Deliver picks the take that carries the line's file name.
  - Hands-free mode: a take starts when you speak and stops after the silence you set.
  - A delivery sheet: a CSV with one row per line, giving its file name, line, direction, length, take, date and a clipped flag.

## Works with
- CSV, TSV, TXT, Excel (.xlsx) and Word (.docx) scripts.
- Numbers files and Google Sheets, once saved as CSV or Excel.
- Any microphone or audio interface the Mac sees.
- Any audio editor that opens WAV files.

Linetake is not made by or affiliated with Microsoft or Google.

## What it does not do
- It does not split one long recording into lines.
- It does not edit audio beyond the silence trim: no gain, noise removal or normalizing.
- It writes mono WAV files only.
- It does not open .xls, .doc or Pages files.
- It has no account, no tracking and no subscription.

## Compared with
- Adobe Audition, $22.99 a month: batch tools add prefixes, suffixes and numbers, but cannot name tracks from a list.
- TwistedWave, $79.90: splits a recording at markers, and you type each name.
- Audacity, free: exports one file per label, but you place and type every label.
- A Better Finder Rename, $29.95: renames files from a spreadsheet, but does not record or match takes to lines.
- voXover and Word2WAV: script recorders for voice actors that are no longer kept up to date.
- Linetake, free for 20 lines or $4.99 once: records each line and names its file from the script.

## Questions
**Can I name my voice over files from a spreadsheet or CSV?**
Yes. Linetake builds each file name from the script's columns, such as ID and Character, as you record.

**Can I record each script line as a separate file?**
Yes. In Linetake, each take saves as its own WAV, named for its line.

**Can Linetake split one long recording into separate files?**
No. Linetake records each line as its own take. Splitting a long take is not part of this version.

**Can I keep several takes of a line?**
Yes, with Linetake Pro. Each replaced take stays in an "Other takes" folder, and Deliver picks the one that carries the line's name.

**Do I need a subscription?**
No. Linetake is free for the first 20 lines of every script. Pro is a one-time purchase of $4.99 in the US.

**Can I record without touching the keyboard?**
Yes, with Linetake Pro. Hands-free mode starts a take when you speak and moves to the next line after the silence.
